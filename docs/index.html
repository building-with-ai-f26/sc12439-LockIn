PRODUCT PAGE · PRD v1

LOCK-IN PARTY

An 8-bit party game where friends lock in 90-day goals (workouts, 10,000 steps, reading, no drinking), check in every day, and evolve their characters by keeping their streaks and Lock-in Power alive.

90 DAYS FRIENDS ONLY ANY GOAL ▶ PLAY

▶ THE PROBLEM

Friend groups start challenges all the time ("let's all work out 3x a week"), but they live in a group chat. After a week or two the messages stop, nobody knows who's still going, and there's no fun reason to keep showing up.

Lock-In Party turns a shared goal into a game: every hit week raises your Lock-in Power and every streak evolves your character, so progress is visible, a little competitive, and actually fun to come back to.

▶ REACHABLE USER

Me and my friend group (5–6 people) who already talk in a group chat and have tried fitness challenges before. Several of us track workouts with Whoop; others want non-fitness goals like reading.

I can reach every one of them today by text, which means I can test with real people from week one.

▶ BEFORE / AFTER

Before

  • Someone proposes a challenge in the group chat.
  • Everyone agrees, but goals are vague.
  • Updates are random screenshots, if any.
  • By week 2 the chat goes quiet and the challenge quietly dies.

After

  • The group creates one 90-day challenge.
  • Each person locks in a specific goal on day 1.
  • Hit weeks raise your Lock-in Power and evolve your character; misses drain it.
  • Everyone can see who's ahead, who's slipping, and what's at stake.

▶ CORE WORKFLOW

  1. Build your character, then join a group. Make your 8-bit self once, then create a group or open an invite link.
  2. Lock in your goal. Write it once, specifically (e.g. "Work out 3x per week" or "Read 20 pages a day"). It can't be edited after lock-in.
  3. Check in. Each week, report whether you hit your goal.
  4. Keep your LP up. Hitting your goal adds Lock-in Power; missing it drains LP and can devolve you.
  5. See who is winning. The leaderboard shows everyone's evolution stage, LP and streak through day 90.

▶ NON-GOALS (v1)

  • ✕No Whoop or wearable integration. Check-ins are self-reported for now. Auto-verifying fitness goals through Whoop is a later build.
  • ✕No minigames. LP, streaks and evolution are the whole game in v1; party-style minigames come later.
  • ✕No public matchmaking. Invite-only friend groups. No strangers, no global leaderboards.

▶ OPEN QUESTION

How do we verify non-fitness goals? Options: honor system, photo proof, or a friend approving each check-in. v1 uses the honor system; I'll watch whether my friends trust it.

▶ PLAY (BUILD 2)

The game loop is live, Pokémon style. Everyone starts at 50 LP (Lock-in Power). Lock in one or more goals: workouts per week, 10,000-step days, reading days, dry days. Check in as you go. Hit every goal in a week, +10 LP. Miss any, -15 LP. Three hit weeks in a row evolves your Orangupe into a Primacho, seven into a Mega-Simian. Break the streak or hit 0 LP and you devolve. Highest LP on day 90 wins. Connect a Whoop and your workouts log themselves every night at 11:59 PM.

▶ START OR JOIN A CHALLENGE ▶ PICK YOUR MONKEY

Onboarding is four steps. Pick your monkey first: everyone is a monkey with one neutral body per stage (Orangupe, Primacho, Mega-Simian), and you choose hair style, fur, hair and outfit colours. Then create or join a group; the system hands the creator an invite code. Then pick your goals, each with its own weekly target, and lock them in with your piece beside them. Finally choose how progress gets recorded: connect a Whoop, Apple Health (coming soon) or tap it in by hand. As your streak grows your monkey evolves through the three stages, with a flame aura at stage 2 and a spirit glow at stage 3, and small badges under it show the goals you locked in.

BUILD 2 · SMALLEST LOCAL VERSION · DUE NOV 16
What works
  • Core workflow end to end at game.html: pick your monkey, create or join a group by invite code, lock in one or more goals (workouts, steps, reading, no drinking), choose Whoop or manual tracking, check in, gain LP, evolve, and see the leaderboard.
  • Shared backend on Supabase free tier. Each phone signs in anonymously; Row Level Security means you can only write your own check-ins and goals. Verified with a two-identity smoke test against the live project.
  • Game rules, sprites, pixelation and the Whoop mapping are pure functions; 52 Node tests pass. Every screen was also screenshotted in headless Chrome against an in-memory fake backend before shipping.
  • Whoop is connected for me and the first real sync ran from GitHub Actions. PostHog is receiving pageviews and the named events.
What I learned
  • Verify, don't assume. Twice a migration "ran" but the live API still returned 404, once because the SQL went into chat instead of the editor. A curl against the REST endpoint takes two seconds and settles it.
  • The Supabase anon key being public is fine; Row Level Security is the real gate. The two-identity smoke test that tries to write someone else's data and gets 403 is the test that matters.
  • Keeping the game math as pure functions paid off: swapping the race board for LP and evolution was a one-hour change with tests, and the UI followed.
  • Third-party APIs have paperwork. Whoop needed a privacy policy URL before it would create the app, rotates refresh tokens on every use, and caps development apps at 10 members. Postgres partial unique indexes can't be targeted by PostgREST's on-conflict, so the sync pre-filters instead.
  • GitHub cron is UTC only. "11:59 PM Eastern all year" means two cron lines plus a timezone guard in the script.
  • A 20-minute minimum made two walks count as workouts on day one. Rules need a real data point before they feel right; now 30 minutes and no walking.
What broke or changed
  • Chose Supabase over localStorage so friends share one board from GitHub Pages. Tradeoff: identity is per device, so switching phones means a new player.
  • Every check-in is its own row with a source column so a Whoop sync or an MCP tool can feed the same table later without touching the rules.
  • Widened from one fitness goal to four goal types after sketching the onboarding: the workouts table became checkins pointing at a goals row, so a dry day and a workout are the same kind of thing to the rules. A week is hit only when every goal is met, which keeps one LP bar and one streak per player. Steps, reading and dry days count once per calendar day. Onboarding gained a fourth step for choosing Whoop, Apple Health (not built; no web API) or manual entry.
  • Groups became manageable: you can be in several and switch from the play screen, and the creator can rename, re-date before day one, remove a member or delete the group. Until now nothing about a group could change because only insert and read policies existed; migration 005 adds creator-only update and delete plus a trigger that freezes the start date once scored weeks exist.
  • Hit weeks score the moment you hit the goal, not at week's end, for faster feedback.
  • Replaced the race board with a Pokémon-style loop after playing the first version: LP as a health bar, evolution at 3 and 7 hit weeks in a row, devolve on a broken streak. One stat to protect reads better on a phone than 40 squares, and evolution gives streaks a visible payoff. Starting LP 50 and faint-reset 25 are first guesses to tune with real players.
  • Replaced the pixelated-photo avatar with a hand-drawn sprite builder after seeing the reference style I actually wanted: flat colours, full body, 15×24 grid. A photo now only suggests colours. Still $0 and browser-only; an AI redraw remains a later experiment.
  • Then replaced the sprite builder with the monkey evolution line for onboarding friends: one generated pixel-art body per stage (Orangupe, Primacho, Mega-Simian), recoloured in the browser by hunting for three reference hues so fur, hair and outfit stay customisable without a file per combination. Hair style is the one trait that needs its own image. Goal badges under the monkey replaced in-hand props because a player can have several goals. Spec for the art in docs/MONKEYS.md; placeholder crops until the generated files land.
  • Whoop sync shipped as a nightly GitHub Action at 11:59 PM Eastern: pulls the last 3 days of workouts of 30+ minutes (walks excluded) and logs them with source = 'whoop', deduped by Whoop id. Tokens live in a service-role-only table, never in the repo. Setup in docs/WHOOP.md.
  • PostHog installed on every page: pageviews and autocapture, plus named events for the moments that matter (character created, group created or joined, goal locked, tracking chosen, check-in logged or undone, week hit, evolved, invite copied). Identified by the anonymous Supabase id only; session recording off. Config in docs/posthog-config.js.
